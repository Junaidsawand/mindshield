## 1. Purpose and Scope
This document provides a comprehensive academic record of the datasets, data pre-processing pipelines, validation strategies, training parameters, and experimental results for the MindShield classification system. It establishes the scientific methodology used to train and evaluate the deep learning model (**`distilbert-pakscam-v1`**) and its experimental candidates (**`v2`** and **`v3`**), ensuring reproducibility and academic rigor for the Final Year Project (FYP-I).

## 2. Dataset Sources
MindShield's training and evaluation utilize two primary dataset sources:
1. **UCI SMS Spam Collection**: A public, standardized corpus used to train the baseline English spam detection model.
2. **PakScam-SMS**: A custom-built, template-based dataset designed to capture Pakistani SMS phishing (smishing) and advance-fee scam characteristics, including Roman Urdu transliteration and code-switching.

## 4. PakScam-SMS Dataset Composition
The custom localized corpus in the final research candidate phase (`v3`) contains **205 unique samples**. 

$$\text{Total Unique Samples} = 140 \text{ (Base Templates)} + 65 \text{ (Manual Augmentations)} = 205$$

The $140$ base templates are partitioned into:
* **$125$ Synthetic Templates**: Created to model localized smishing patterns (fake lottery schemes, government aid impersonations).
* **$15$ Real-World Seeds**: Real-world user-observed smishing SMS received in Pakistan.
* **Important**: The 15 real-world seeds are **included within the 140 base templates** (and are not counted in addition).

## 6. Language / Linguistic Characteristics
South Asian telecommunications fraud utilizes bilingual linguistic patterns:
1. **Roman Urdu**: Urdu text written in the Latin alphabet (e.g., *"apka account suspend ho gaya hai"*).
2. **English-Urdu Code-Switching**: Alternating between English terms and Urdu structures (e.g., *"HBL Alerts: Account blocked, restore karne ke liye call karein"*).
3. **Local Bank Names**: Impersonating institutions like HBL, UBL, Meezan Bank, and Allied Bank.
4. **Mobile Wallet Targets**: Impersonating JazzCash, Easypaisa, NayaPay, and SadaPay.
5. **Government Aid Impersonation**: Ehsaas Program, Benazir Income Support Program (BISP).
6. **Shortened Shortlinks**: Using services like `bit.ly` or `tinyurl.com` to bypass standard domain filters.

---

## 7. Real-World Seed Samples
The 15 user-observed seed records are authentic scam SMS captured in Pakistan:
* *"BISP: Aap ka 25,000 ka Ehsaas cash approved ho gaya hai. Verify CNIC: http://bit.ly/ehsaas-claim"* (Impersonating BISP).
* *"JazzCash: Your account PIN has been reset. If you didn't request this, contact support immediately: 0300-1122334"* (Social Engineering).
* *"Meezan Bank Alert: Transaction of Rs 85,000 pending. Cancel here: bit.ly/meezan-cancel"* (Credential Harvesting).

---

## 8. Synthetic Template Generation
To expand the corpus, 125 synthetic templates were generated by modifying parameters in the real-world seeds (e.g., switching bank names from HBL to UBL, altering transaction amounts, using different mobile network names). This approach helps the model generalize across variations of the same scam format.

---

## 9. V3 Augmentation Strategy
The 65 manual augmentations in `v3` target specific model classification weaknesses (false positives):
* **G1: bit.ly banking HAM (15)**: Benign banking alerts containing `bit.ly` shortlinks (e.g., customer support feedback).
* **G2: bit.ly telecom HAM (5)**: Official carrier promotions containing links.
* **G3: bit.ly service HAM (5)**: Utility bills and parcel tracking notifications.
* **G4: flash-sale promotions HAM (8)**: Legitimate limited-time network discounts.
* **G5: VAS subscription notifications HAM (7)**: Standard carrier billing alerts.
* **G6: Roman Urdu HAM patch (15)**: Benign transactional Roman Urdu messages.
* **G7: Hard SPAM - Roman Urdu + bit.ly (5)**: Highly targeted smishing threats.
* **G8: Hard SPAM - Social Engineering (5)**: Impersonation attacks without links.

## 11. Data Cleaning and Preprocessing
The preprocessing pipeline is executed inside [`pipeline.py`](file:///c:/Users/n%20tech/OneDrive/Desktop/mindshield/backend/app/nlp/pipeline.py):
1. **Unicode Normalisation**: Normalises inputs using NFKC.
2. **Lowercasing**: Converts characters to lowercase to prevent word-casing bias.
3. **Regex Token Normalisation**: Normalises specific targets:
   * Shortlinks and domains are normalized to `[url]`.
   * Telephone numbers are normalized to `[phone]`.
   * Numeric values (OTP codes, transaction amounts) are normalized to `[number]`.

---

## 12. PII / Privacy Considerations
To protect user privacy, the dataset does not include real phone numbers or names. Preprocessing normalizes all phone numbers to `[phone]`. During production log operations, the backend service only records the classification label, confidence score, and processing latency; no sender phone numbers are written to disk.

## 14. Oversampling
Because the UCI spam dataset is much larger than PakScam-SMS, combining them directly would cause the model to ignore localized smishing signals. To address this class imbalance, MindShield implements the **Mix-C oversampling strategy**:
* Localized train records are oversampled **3x** before being combined with the UCI train set.
* This ensures that localized smishing features (like Roman Urdu and carrier billing keywords) receive sufficient weight during backpropagation.

---

## 15. Data Leakage Prevention
To prevent data leakage during splitting:
* **Template-Family Grouping**: Splits are grouped at the template-family level (using `template_family` labels) rather than splitting randomly.
* **Leakage Verification**: The training script verifies that no template families overlap between the train and test splits, ensuring the model is evaluated on unseen scam campaigns.

## 17. Model Training Methodology
* **Base Model**: `distilbert-base-uncased` (255MB, 66 million parameters).
* **Optimizer**: AdamW.
* **Learning Rate**: $2 \times 10^{-5}$.
* **Batch Size**: 32.
* **Max Sequence Length**: 128 tokens.
* **Epochs**: 4.
* **Patience**: 3 (Early stopping).

---

## 18. Evaluation Methodology
Models are evaluated on three subsets:
1. **UCI Test Split**: Standard English spam collection test split (515 samples).
2. **PakScam Test Split**: Custom localized test templates.
3. **14-Case Hard-Negative Regression Suite**: A set of 14 complex, benign messages containing trigger words (like `bit.ly` links or transaction terms) used to check for false positive regressions.

---

## 19. Metrics

### 19.1 Mathematical Formulations

$$\text{Precision} = \frac{TP}{TP + FP}$$

$$\text{Recall} = \frac{TP}{TP + FN}$$

$$F_1 = 2 \times \frac{\text{Precision} \times \text{Recall}}{\text{Precision} + \text{Recall}}$$

$$\text{FPR} = \frac{FP}{FP + TN}$$

## 23. Dataset Limitations

### 23.1 Template-Derived Bias
The localized dataset is primarily template-derived (205 unique samples). This limits:
* **Population Diversity**: May not cover all formatting and typing variations in real-world SMS.
* **Linguistic Diversity**: Vocabulary is limited to base keywords.
* **Adversarial Variation**: Vulnerable to intentional misspellings (e.g. `J@zzCash`).

### 23.2 Temporal Drift
Telecom fraud campaigns evolve rapidly. Models trained on historical templates may see accuracy declines when new scam campaigns emerge.

---

## 24. Ethical Considerations
* **Biased Alert Risks**: High false-positive rates can cause users to block legitimate banking alerts, leading to missed notifications.
* **False Safety Guarantees**: No model achieves 100% accuracy. The UI must clearly convey that the app is an advisory tool, not a guarantee of safety.
